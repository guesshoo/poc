In Raft (and most leader-based consensus protocols) a **term** is simply a monotonically increasing epoch number that identifies “rounds” of leader election.  Here’s the nutshell:

1. **What a term represents**

   * Whenever the cluster needs a new leader (e.g. on startup, or after a leader crashes/partition), it starts an election in a new term.
   * Terms start at 0 (or 1) and go up by one each time an election is triggered.

2. **Why you need to track `current_term`**

   * **Safety:** a server in term 7 will refuse to accept any messages from a server claiming to be leader for term 6 or lower.
   * **Election logic:** when you receive a RequestVote RPC with a higher term, you update your own term and step down if you’re currently leader.
   * **Persistence:** you must persist the highest term you’ve seen (in stable storage) so that after a crash you won’t accidentally revert to an older term and violate safety.

3. **How to store it**

   * Just like the log “last index,” you persist `current_term` (a simple `u64`) in your metadata bucket in LMDB (e.g. under key `"current_term"` → 8-byte BE).
   * You also include the term in each WAL entry header (`[term][index][…]`) so that, on recovery, you can pick up both the last index and the last term you’d written.

4. **In memory vs on disk**

   * **In memory:** keep a `u64 current_term` alongside your `last_log_index`.
   * **On disk:** after you bump the term (e.g. starting a new election) or see a higher term from another server, do an LMDB txn to update `"current_term"` before replying to any RPC.

---

So when I said “current term” I meant that per-Raft you need to track and persist the latest election epoch number (a `u64`) just like you track the latest log index—both in your WAL entries and in your LMDB metadata—so that after crashes or restarts you resume in the correct consensus state.
